Books from Around the World

An A–Z reading list — one book for every letter, by an author from a country whose name starts with it.

A small project: travel the alphabet by way of the globe. For each letter I picked a country that begins with it, then a book by an author from that country. Suggestions and corrections are very welcome.

A
Argentina
Ficciones — Jorge Luis Borges

Labyrinths, infinite libraries, and imaginary books reviewed as if they existed.

B
Brazil
Dom Casmurro — Machado de Assis

An unreliable narrator picks apart his own jealousy in 19th-century Rio.

C
Colombia
One Hundred Years of Solitude — Gabriel García Márquez

The Buendía family and the town of Macondo, where the magical is matter-of-fact.

D
Denmark
Fairy Tales — Hans Christian Andersen

The originals behind “The Little Mermaid” and “The Snow Queen,” darker than you remember.

E
Egypt
Palace Walk — Naguib Mahfouz

First of the Cairo Trilogy by the Arab world’s first Nobel laureate in literature.

F
France
The Stranger — Albert Camus

A flat Mediterranean light, a senseless killing, and the absurd laid bare.

G
Germany
The Tin Drum — Günter Grass

A boy who refuses to grow up drums his way through the rise of Nazism.

H
Hungary
Fatelessness — Imre Kertész

A teenager’s eerily matter-of-fact account of the camps, by a survivor and Nobel laureate.

I
India
The God of Small Things — Arundhati Roy

Twins, caste, and a single day that reshapes a family in Kerala.

J
Japan
The Wind-Up Bird Chronicle — Haruki Murakami

A missing cat pulls an ordinary man into dry wells and parallel Tokyos.

K
Kenya
Weep Not, Child — Ngũgĩ wa Thiong’o

A boy’s schooling and the Mau Mau uprising under British colonial rule.

L
Lebanon
The Prophet — Kahlil Gibran

Lyric meditations on love, work, and freedom from one of the best-selling poets ever.

M
Mexico
Pedro Páramo — Juan Rulfo

A son searches for his father in a town that turns out to be full of ghosts.

N
Nigeria
Things Fall Apart — Chinua Achebe

The Igbo world of Okonkwo, and the arrival of colonialism that undoes it.

O
Oman
Celestial Bodies — Jokha Alharthi

Three sisters and a changing Oman; the first Arabic novel to win the International Booker.

P
Portugal
Blindness — José Saramago

An epidemic of white blindness strips a city to its rawest instincts.

Q
Qatar
The Corsair — Abdulaziz Al Mahmoud

A 19th-century Gulf adventure of pirates and empire — a landmark Qatari novel.

R
Russia
Crime and Punishment — Fyodor Dostoevsky

A student’s theory of the “extraordinary man” collapses into guilt.

S
Spain
Don Quixote — Miguel de Cervantes

The knight of the woeful countenance tilts at windmills; often called the first modern novel.

T
Turkey
My Name Is Red — Orhan Pamuk

A murder mystery among Ottoman miniaturists, narrated even by a coin and a color.

U
Uganda
Kintu — Jennifer Nansubuga Makumbi

A family curse traced across centuries of Ugandan history.

V
Vietnam
The Tale of Kiều — Nguyễn Du

Vietnam’s national epic poem: a daughter’s sacrifice across years of misfortune.

W
Wales
Under Milk Wood — Dylan Thomas

A day in a Welsh seaside town, told as a “play for voices.”

X
—
No sovereign country begins with X

A gap on the map. If you know a great book tied to a place starting with X, tell me and I’ll add it.

Y
Yemen
They Die Strangers — Mohammad Abdul-Wali

Stories of Yemeni emigrants and exile from a pioneering modern Yemeni writer.

Z
Zimbabwe
Nervous Conditions — Tsitsi Dangarembga

A girl’s education and the weight of colonialism and gender in 1960s Rhodesia.
